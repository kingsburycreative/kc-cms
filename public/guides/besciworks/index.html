Editing the BeSciWorks website

This guide shows you how to change the BeSciWorks website yourself: adding Weekly posts and papers, editing the text on a page, checking how the site shows up in search, and undoing a change if something goes wrong.

For David Mills and the BeSciWorks team. Prepared by Kingsbury Creative, October 2026.

Everything you save goes live on the website about a minute later. There is no separate publish button, so save only when you're happy for people to see it. If you save something by mistake, Undo a change shows how to put it back.

1. Sign in

You don't need a password. The editor emails you a six-digit code each time you sign in.

The first email you get is an invitation from Kingsbury Creative, with the subject You're invited to edit BeSciWorks and a button marked Edit BeSciWorks. You can use that button, or just follow these steps with the same email address.

  1. Go to cms.kingsburycreative.com/besciworks. Bookmark this address, because it's the one you'll use every time.
  2. Type the email address we set up for you and choose Continue with email.
  3. Check your inbox for an email from Kingsbury Creative with the code. It can take a minute to arrive, and it may land in your junk folder the first time.
  4. Type the code into the box and sign in. The code stops working after 5 minutes, so if it has expired, ask for a new one.

After a few wrong codes in a row the editor makes you wait before you can try again. That's a security measure. Wait a few minutes, then ask for a fresh code.

2. Find your way around

After you sign in you land on the dashboard. It's the front page of the editor: it shows how the site is doing and has buttons for the jobs you do most.

From the top down, the dashboard shows:

  • The site name and See live site. The name of your site is at the top. See live site opens the BeSciWorks website in a new tab.
  • Quick actions. New Weekly post, New paper, New Insight Brief and Upload a PDF take you straight to a blank form or the upload screen.
  • Visits. A chart of how many people visited, which you can show for 7, 30 or 90 days, with tiles beside it for visitors, pages viewed and time on site. See who visits has more.
  • Recent changes. Who changed what, and when. Choose See all changes under the list to see every change on the site.
  • Search health. Pages that need a fix so Google can describe them properly. Check search health explains it.
  • Most viewed pages and Where visitors come from. The pages people read most, and the towns they visit from.

The menu runs down the left side of every screen, so you can jump to another part of the site at any time. On a phone, choose the menu button at the top left to open it.

The collections come first: Weekly, What Works, Insight Briefs and Papers. Each holds many entries, you can add new ones, and each entry gets its own page on the site. Media holds the PDF files for your papers.

The single pages, such as the Home page and About page, are folded under one entry called Pages. Choose it to open the list, then pick the page you want. You can change the text on these pages, but you can't add or remove the page itself. The short introductions at the top of the Weekly, What Works, Insight Briefs and Papers pages are in there too.

3. Add a Weekly post

These steps work the same way for What Works and Insight Briefs. Only the name in the list changes.

The fast way in is the New Weekly post button on the dashboard. It opens a blank post, so you can skip to step 3.

  1. Choose Weekly in the list on the left.
  2. Choose Add an entry at the top right.
  3. Fill in the Title. This becomes the heading of the post and part of its web address.
  4. Write the Description. It's the short summary Google shows under your link in search results, and it must be between 120 and 155 characters. The box tells you if it's too short or too long.
  5. Check the Date. It fills in today's date for you.
  6. Under Authors, choose Add an item and type each author's name.
  7. Write or paste the post into the Text box. Format the text shows how to add headings, links and lists.
  8. Choose Save at the top right.

If something is missing or in the wrong format, the box turns red and a message under it says what to fix. Nothing is saved until every red box is fixed.

Changing a post later

Open the collection, choose the post's title in the list, make your change and save. When you correct a post that's already been published, change the Updated date as well, so readers can see it was revised.

References and abbreviations

References adds a reference list at the end of the post: author, year, title and, if there is one, a link or DOI. Abbreviations lets you spell out a short form (for example OECD) once, and the site shows the full name to readers who hover over it or use a screen reader.

4. Format the text

The Text box uses a few simple symbols for formatting. Type them as shown, and the website turns them into headings, links and lists when it publishes.

To getType
A heading## Why this matters on its own line
A smaller heading### What we found
Bold**important words**
Italics*a book title*
A link[Statistics Canada](https://www.statcan.gc.ca)
A bulleted listStart each line with -
A numbered listStart each line with 1. , 2. and so on
A quotationStart the line with >
A footnote[^1] in the sentence, then [^1]: The note. at the end of the text

Leave a blank line between paragraphs. Pasting from Word works, but bold, italics and links won't carry over, so add them again with the symbols above.

5. Add a paper and its PDF

Each paper has its own page on the site, with its abstract, citation details and a download link for the PDF.

  1. Choose Papers in the list on the left, then Add an entry. The New paper button on the dashboard gets you to the same blank form in one step.
  2. Fill in the Title, then add each author in order under Authors, in order. Their ORCID and affiliation are optional.
  3. Choose the Type and the Status. Every type except a preprint needs the journal, publisher or series filled in.
  4. Fill in whatever citation details you have: volume, issue, pages, a link, ISBN or ISSN, keywords and JEL codes. Leave the rest blank.
  5. Paste the Abstract.
  6. Under PDF, choose Upload and pick the file from your computer. It has to be a PDF of 25 MB or less. Wait until the file name appears in the box.
  7. Add the DOI if the paper has one, then choose Save.

The file is renamed when it uploads: Mills Paper 2026 Final.PDF becomes mills-paper-2026-final.pdf. That's on purpose, so the download link works everywhere.

To use a PDF you've already uploaded, choose Select instead of Upload and pick it from the list. The Upload a PDF button on the dashboard adds a file to Media without starting a paper, which helps when you want the file there before you write the entry.

6. Change the text on a page

  1. Choose Pages in the list on the left to open it, then choose the page, for example Home page.
  2. Each box is labelled with the part of the page it controls: the heading, the paragraph under it, a button's wording and so on. Change the words you want.
  3. Choose Save.

The Page title (50 to 60 characters) is what shows in the browser tab and as the blue link in Google. The Description is the summary under that link. Both affect how the page appears in search, so change them with care.

Longer writing on Our Approach

Most pages have short boxes for headings and a few lines of text. Our Approach also has a Main text box for longer writing, such as the Theory of Change. It works like the Text box in a Weekly post, so Format the text shows how to add headings, links and lists.

7. Undo a change

Every save is kept, so nothing you change is lost for good.

  1. Open the post or page you want to put back.
  2. Choose the clock button at the top right, next to Save. It lists the most recent changes and who made them.
  3. Choose the change you want to go back to. If it isn't in the short list, choose See all changes to see every change on the site.
  4. The change opens and shows the wording as it was then. Choose Restore this version.
  5. The editor opens with the older wording filled in. Check it, then choose Save. It goes live again about a minute later.

Recent changes on the dashboard shows the latest changes across the whole site, and See all changes under it lists every one, newest first.

8. Remove a post or paper

  1. Open the post or paper.
  2. Choose the three dots at the top right, then Delete, and confirm.

Deleting a paper leaves its PDF in Media. To remove the file as well, open Media, choose the three dots next to the file and delete it there.

Avoid Rename on anything already published. Renaming changes the page's web address, so links people have shared or saved stop working. If a title needs to change, edit the Title box and leave the name alone.

9. See who visits

Choose Insights to see how many people visited, the pages they read most, roughly where they are, and how they found the site. The charts can show the last 7, 30 or 90 days: choose the number of days above the chart. Move your pointer over the chart (or touch it on a phone) to see the visits for a single day.

The visitor count includes everyone. Numbers from visitors who allowed analytics are marked From visitors who allowed analytics. They count only people who said yes to the cookie card, so they are lower than the visitor count.

10. Check search health

When someone searches for BeSciWorks, Google shows a page's title and a short description under its link. Search health on the dashboard checks that each page has them, and that the headings and picture descriptions on the page are in order. It shows how many pages pass, for example 12 of 14.

The pages to fix are the ones that need attention first, and the list shows at most three. Each has a short reason beside it, such as No description. Pages that pass are folded away. Choose the line that counts them to see which ones they are.

You fix a page on the page itself. Choose its name in the list, fill in the box the reason points to (for a missing description, the Description box, which has to be 120 to 155 characters), and choose Save.

Below the list, a note may say that some posts are hard to read. That is advice for easier reading and does not count against the score. Papers aren't part of this check. If the panel says it couldn't check your pages, your site is still live, so try again in a few minutes.

11. Getting help

If something won't save, looks wrong on the site, or you'd like a new field or page added, email Rob at Kingsbury Creative: hello@kingsburycreative.com. A screenshot of the screen you're on helps.

You can't break the website from the editor. If a change has a problem the site can't publish, the last working version stays live. A wrong edit can be put back with the clock button on the page, and if you delete something by accident, email Rob and he can restore it.
